# Outline assessment: Local Politics Project

## Overall assessment

The document is a section skeleton, not yet an assessable research outline. It provides a generic title, names ANES as the data source, and lists standard sections, but it does not state a research question, argument, outcome, explanatory variables, design, expected contribution, or proposed evidence. **Recommendation: major development required before substantive assessment.**

## What can be assessed

- The conventional section order is serviceable as a starting container.
- Naming ANES narrows the possible empirical domain, but not enough to determine the project topic or feasibility.
- The missing abstract correctly signals that no summary has yet been supplied.

## Blocking omissions

1. **Research question:** State one precise question about local politics that the project will answer.
2. **Contribution:** Identify the relevant scholarly conversation and what this study adds.
3. **Argument or objective:** For an explanatory study, provide a mechanism and hypotheses; for a descriptive study, define the quantities to be documented.
4. **Population and period:** Specify which ANES sample, years, respondents, and geographic coverage are relevant.
5. **Variables:** Define the main outcome, explanatory variables, controls, and how each is measured.
6. **Research design:** Explain whether the analysis is descriptive, associational, or causal and identify the comparison or estimation strategy.
7. **Feasibility:** Verify that the selected ANES waves contain the required local-politics measures and usable geographic identifiers at the needed level.
8. **Results plan:** Name the primary table, figure, or estimate and the criterion for answering the research question.
9. **Limitations:** Anticipate survey measurement, nonresponse, weighting, geographic disclosure, and external-validity constraints.
10. **Reproducibility:** State what code, variable construction, and legally shareable materials will be provided.

## Minimum viable revised outline

### Abstract

In one paragraph, state the question, motivation, data and years, analytical approach, contribution, principal result if known, and inference boundary.

### Introduction

Motivate the specific problem, state the question in one sentence, summarize the answer or planned test, and list no more than two concrete contributions.

### Prior research and expectations

Organize literature around the question rather than summarizing studies generally. End with hypotheses or explicit descriptive expectations tied to observable measures.

### Data and measurement

Identify ANES waves and sample restrictions, define all core variables, explain survey weights, and document missingness and geographic availability.

### Research design

Define the estimand or descriptive target, model, comparison, uncertainty calculation, and major threats to interpretation.

### Results

Begin with the primary estimate or descriptive quantity, then diagnostics and a limited set of pre-specified subgroup or robustness analyses.

### Conclusion

Answer the research question, state what the evidence does not establish, and explain the substantive implication without exceeding the design.

## Bottom line

No claim about the project's rigor or likely findings is possible from the current text. The next useful step is to write the research question and verify the required ANES variables; those two decisions determine the rest of the outline.
